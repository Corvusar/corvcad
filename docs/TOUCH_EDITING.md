# Touch editing on iPad

1. Create a project with **New project** on the home screen, or open one from **Local projects**. The **Schematic** workspace opens with the **Select**, **Part**, **Wire**, and **Label** tools on the floating rail at the left. Choose **Part** and tap an empty grid point. A second part cannot be placed on top of an existing symbol.
2. Choose **Wire**. As you touch or drag across the sheet, a reticle marks the 2.54 mm grid point or a nearby pin, wire endpoint, junction, or label. The bottom readout shows the millimetre coordinates and snap reason. Release at a pin end to start, then release at the destination. The gold dashed preview shows the route the engine will write. It uses right-angle segments and steps around CorvCAD part bodies, pin ends, wire ends, junctions, and labels, because KiCad joins any of those that lie on a wire. If no clear route exists, the preview turns red and the wire is rejected; route it in shorter steps. **Cancel** in the top chip discards the pending start. Snapping to a wire crossing or midpoint is not implemented.
3. Choose **Label**, type the net name in the top chip, and tap a pin or wire endpoint. Choose **Select** to inspect. In Select, tap a wire, label or pin end to highlight its net. Its wires glow, its pins are marked, everything else dims, and a chip shows the net's KiCad-style name and pin count. Tap **Clear** or empty space to end the highlight. Hold a symbol or label to open the orbit menu with **Move / Rename / Delete**. Either keep the finger down, slide onto an action and release, or lift and tap an action. Tap outside to close. After choosing Move, tap the new position. Hold a wire for Delete. Delete takes effect immediately with no confirmation; use **Undo** in the top bar (⌘Z on a keyboard) to restore it. Renaming a schematic symbol changes its placed reference and project instance reference.
4. Switch to **PCB** in the top mode switcher. Choose **Part**, enter a reference and value in the top chip, then tap inside the outline. Choose **M3** to add a 3.2 mm non-plated hole; it feeds the enclosure preview. PCB placement and moves snap to a 0.5 mm grid. Hold a footprint or mounting hole for **Move / Rename / Delete**. The layers button at the top right filters copper layers and nets.
5. To draw copper, choose **Track** and pick **F.Cu** or **B.Cu** in the top chip. Tap a pad, track end or via to start; the track takes that item's net, which the chip shows.
   - A tap in free space adds a corner on the 0.5 mm grid. It lines up with the previous point, or with a pad, track end or via within 0.75 mm. The track carries on from that corner.
   - Ending on a pad, track end or via finishes the track; **Done** stops it early.
   - **Via** drops a 0.6 mm via (0.3 mm drill) on a tapped pad or track end, joining F.Cu and B.Cu.
   - Tracks are 0.25 mm wide. Hold a track or via for **Delete**.

   CorvCAD does not check clearances or shorts while you draw. A track started on one net can cross another, and the CorvCAD starter pads exist only on F.Cu, so a B.Cu track needs a via to reach them. Run KiCad DRC.
6. Projects with hierarchical sheets show each child sheet as a gold box named after its sheet. In **Select**, tap a box to open that sheet; the breadcrumb at the top left returns to a parent. Parts, wires and labels are edited on the sheet you are viewing. Adding a part to a sheet file used by more than one sheet instance is refused, as is renaming a reference there, because KiCad stores per-instance references that this editor does not yet write. A sheet using constructs newer than KiCad 10.0.6 or unknown to the reader opens read-only with a notice.
7. **Undo** and **Redo** in the top bar (⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z) step back and forward through edits to the project's KiCad files in the order they were made. Schematic steps use the engine session's transaction history; board steps restore the board file's previous text. History holds up to 50 steps and is cleared when you open another project or import a file. Enclosure slider changes are not part of it.
8. The inspector (right-hand button in the top bar) holds numeric symbol and footprint moves, Value/Footprint property edits, enclosure parameters, and STL export. The share button exports the KiCad files.
9. In Files, copy the entire folder from **On My iPad → CorvCAD → Projects** to the PC. Include `.kicad_pro`, `.kicad_sch`, `.kicad_pcb`, `sym-lib-table`, `fp-lib-table`, `CorvCAD.kicad_sym`, and `CorvCAD.pretty/`. Run KiCad ERC and DRC before fabrication.

These touch operations work on local project files. Individually imported files are read-only for additions and orbit-menu edits; the inspector's move controls can save shareable copies. The engine edits selected KiCad expressions while preserving unrelated source text. Schematic edits run as session transactions: each one is saved immediately by staging the changed sheet files, refusing the save if a file changed on disk since it was opened, and restoring backups on a reported failure. Board edits use atomic file replacement.

The schematic and board are synchronized only on request. In **PCB**, **Update from schematic** reads the saved root schematic and does three things:
- it gives each footprint's pads the nets of the schematic part with the same reference;
- it clears pad nets that no longer exist;
- it adds a missing CorvCAD two-pad footprint at a free spot inside the outline.

An alert says what it added or could not update, and Undo reverts the whole step. It leaves board-only footprints such as mounting holes alone. It also leaves footprints whose library ID differs from the schematic's, and it refuses hierarchical projects. Nothing happens automatically: renaming or deleting a schematic part does not update its board footprint, moving a symbol does not reroute wires, and deleting a footprint does not remove attached tracks. The schematic preview knows pin positions only for CorvCAD's unrotated two-pin symbol. Use KiCad ERC/DRC to find unfinished connections and manufacturing errors. The starter two-pad geometry is not a verified package recommendation.
